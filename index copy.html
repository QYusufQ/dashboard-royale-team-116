The Two Humps

Low-performing machines cluster primarily in Back Forty and a handful of game themes.

Evidence

Location effect: Machines in the Back Forty zone have 35% lower occupancy than the floor average, driving down coin-in regardless of machine theme.

Low-stakes design: Games like Comet Cash and Lucky Lanterns have normal occupancy rates but lower bet caps ($1 max) that reduce total dollar play.

Noise vs. underperformance: Only a small fraction of machine-level variance comes from the machine itself. Most "underperformance" is driven by placement, traffic, and bet limits.

Decision

Reallocate by traffic: Move the strongest games out of Back Forty into high-traffic zones, and use Back Forty for destination games that pull players in.

Place low-stakes games where seats fill: Keep high-occupancy, low-bet games in high-foot-traffic areas to maximize utilization.

Avoid hasty replacements: Don't spend budget replacing 10 machines; replacement themes (like Royal Draw) yield minor improvements that don't pay back the cost.

♦ Demand Analysis

What changed in demand across the casino floor?

Key Finding

Low-performing areas like the Back Forty suffer because their seats sit empty 35% more than the floor average, while seated players bet at normal rates.

Why Zones Earn Less: Occupancy vs Bet Size

Occupancy and average bet per zone, indexed to floor average = 100. Back Forty falls on occupancy, not bet size: an empty-seat problem, not a player problem.

Zone Traffic

Evidence

The heatmap shows physical location heavily drives play frequency, with zones like the Back Forty generating significantly lower coin-in per available hour than high-traffic areas like the Main Entrance. Games with low total earnings (such as Comet Cash and Lucky Lanterns) maintain solid occupancy in certain zones, showing they are popular low-stakes options rather than broken or unwanted games.
